# Strategy as an Organizational Layer

The statement below is a synthesis, not a direct claim from one source. It
should not be attributed to a single paper.

## Closest References

- Herd et al. (2013), [“Strategic cognitive sequencing: a computational
  cognitive neuroscience approach”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23935605/),
  describes strategy as an “outer loop” that decides which cognitive process
  to apply at each point in a complex, multistep task. It connects goals,
  planning, prediction of reward, and selection among strategies.
- Fuster and Bressler (2015), [“Past makes future: role of PFC in
  prediction”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25321486/), argues that planning,
  prediction, decision-making, action, and error monitoring are dynamically
  intertwined in goal-directed behavior.
- Rangel, Camerer, and Montague (2008), [“A framework for studying the
  neurobiology of value-based decision
  making”](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2357), models goal-directed
  decision-making as representation, action valuation, action selection,
  outcome valuation, and learning.
- Ullsperger et al. (2014), [“Neurophysiology of performance monitoring and
  adaptive behavior”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24382883/), explains how
  performance monitoring and feedback allow actions and decisions to be
  evaluated, corrected, and adapted toward goals.

## Defensible Formulation for the Article

> Strategy is the goal-directed coordination of cognitive operations and
> actions over time. It can combine inference, prediction, planning, valuation,
> action selection, and revision in response to feedback.

This wording makes clear that the formulation is a functional synthesis
supported collectively by the sources—not a settled neuroscientific definition
or a quotation from one study. Strategy is best understood as an organizational
layer that employs other forms of reasoning.
