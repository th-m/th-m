# Review 1: Draft 4

Reviewed: 2026-08-21

Source: [`draft 4.md`](../draft/draft%204.md)

## Overall Assessment

The draft has an engaging opening and a promising human/AI comparison, but it
is not yet publication-ready. The central argument currently overreaches its
evidence and never fully delivers the promised framework for solutions,
meaning, and value.

## Main Findings

### 1. Correct the HBR results

The HBR results are mischaracterized. The 2% result applied only to particular
strategy tensions, not to deeper analysis generally. More importantly, the
study examined strategic tradeoffs rather than questions with one objectively
correct answer.

The defensible finding is that the tested recommendations were surprisingly
context-insensitive and order-sensitive. The existing
[`research-review.md`](../research/research-review.md) states the result more
accurately and should govern the publication wording.

### 2. Define the AI system boundary

The draft compares a base model even though its opening evaluates an agentic
system involving Goal mode, repeated execution, documents, and presumably tool
use. Those surrounding components are part of the system being evaluated.

Tools cannot simply be omitted because both humans and AI can use them. Memory,
feedback, controllers, verification, permissions, and stopping conditions
materially determine what problems an AI system can solve.

### 3. Separate tokenization from phenomenal capture

The tokenizer-to-qualia argument conflates several issues:

- BPE is one tokenization family, not the origin of tokenization generally.
- Tokenization segments information that has already been captured.
- Any absence of phenomenal information from a record occurs before
  tokenization, during experience, measurement, description, and capture.
- Morphemes are linguistic units carrying meaning or grammatical function, not
  a general mechanism for compressing experience.

The article should distinguish statistical, semantic, functional, phenomenal,
and normative meaning rather than treating them as one category.

### 4. Do not infer ineffability from incomplete neuroscience

Incomplete and heterogeneous accounts of human learning do not make its
mechanisms ineffable or undecidable. The cited research also does not
demonstrate that learning is inseparable from conscious phenomena; unconscious
learning is an important counterexample.

A more defensible claim is that human learning is active, embodied, socially
situated, affectively weighted, and distributed across multiple timescales.

### 5. Narrow the claim about evaluating meaning

The statement that AI has no mechanism for evaluating meaning is too absolute.
AI systems can use probabilities, reward models, supplied rubrics, critics,
tests, environmental feedback, and human evaluation.

The stronger distinction is:

> An AI can apply supplied criteria, but it does not independently establish
> which criteria represent human stakes or deserve normative authority.

### 6. Keep the creativity evidence within scope

The creativity studies demonstrate patterns, not the proposed consciousness
mechanism.

- *Weird Corners* studied one convergent interaction design with nine expert
  creatives. It supports a bounded workflow claim about premature convergence,
  not a general AI incapacity for meaning or creativity.
- *StoryScope* provides substantial evidence that the five tested models
  clustered within a shared narrative space, but it does not show that a lack
  of qualia caused that convergence.

Use these as case studies of observed convergence rather than proof of an
underlying consciousness-based limitation.

### 7. Fulfill the reader promise

The article asks what kinds of problems AI is equipped to solve, but it ends
before answering that question. It still needs:

1. definitions of *solution*, *meaning*, and *value*;
2. a clear distinction between a model and a surrounding AI system;
3. a practical problem-fit framework; and
4. a conclusion that returns to the failed planning session.

The blanket statement that humans are more capable than AI should also become
task-specific. Human cognition is broader, embodied, situated, and continuous,
while AI can nevertheless exceed human performance on some bounded tasks.

## Recommended Thesis

The strongest thesis is already present in Draft 3:

> An AI output becomes a solution only inside a larger loop that identifies a
> problem, supplies a valued outcome, evaluates consequences, and revises its
> judgment. Task fit depends on which context, memory, feedback, and value
> criteria the surrounding system provides—and which remain human
> responsibilities.

Restore this thesis, compress the cognitive comparison, and use creativity and
strategy as bounded case studies rather than as proof of a
consciousness-based limitation.
