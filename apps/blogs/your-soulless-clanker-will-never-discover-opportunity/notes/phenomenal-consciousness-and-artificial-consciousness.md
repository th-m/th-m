# Phenomenal Consciousness and Artificial Consciousness

## Drafting question

What do you mean when you say consciousness? Immediately after explaining how none of these theories prove consciousness, you say “possibly artificial consciousness.” How could we prove consciousness in an AI before we have defined it for ourselves? That sounds like semantics, with AI using the word to mean something entirely different.

## Working answer

When this post says **consciousness**, it means **phenomenal consciousness**: whether there is anything it is like to be a system. It does not mean intelligence, fluent self-report, global information access, or the ability to use the words “I feel.”

We do not have an agreed definition that turns phenomenal consciousness into a measurable property—not even for humans. For other people, we make a powerful inference from our own first-person experience, shared biological architecture, and convergent evidence from behavior, development, injury, anesthesia, and report.

For AI, that shared-organism bridge is absent. A model saying “I feel pain” establishes only that it can generate that sentence. It does not establish a private phenomenal referent behind *feel* or *pain*. This is not a trivial semantic quibble; it is the central epistemic problem.

> We do not yet possess a settled account of what phenomenal consciousness is, much less a test that establishes it in an artificial system. Theories of artificial consciousness are conditional proposals about what would count, not evidence that current language models have an inner life.

So we cannot presently prove AI phenomenal consciousness. At most, a theory could offer a conditional standard:

> If phenomenal consciousness is constituted by X causal organization, and an artificial system demonstrably instantiates X, then that system should count as conscious under that theory.

That is not proof in the ordinary sense. It is a theory-dependent attribution. “Possible artificial consciousness” should therefore mean only **metaphysically or theoretically possible**, not “current AI may be conscious” and not “we know how to verify it.”

## Editorial implication

An LLM's facility with the vocabulary of experience is not evidence that it possesses the thing the vocabulary ordinarily points to. Until there is both a settled target and a validated bridge from system properties to phenomenal experience, treating a current language model as an experiencing stakeholder is unsupported projection rather than an evidence-based conclusion.
