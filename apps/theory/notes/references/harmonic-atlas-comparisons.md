Yes—but the market is fragmented. I found no established product that combines the entire Harmonic Atlas idea: a beautiful phone-first harmony map that is simultaneously playable, compositional, layered, educational, and adaptive.

The strongest opportunity is that Sound Sculpt could make the same graph serve three purposes:

1. **Learn:** see why chords relate and hear every relationship.
2. **Compose:** trace routes, arrange them beside one another and stack maps into layers.
3. **Adapt:** turn unused edges into permitted alternatives while preserving anchors and convergence points.

That last part is especially differentiated. Existing products generally separate the friendly composing interface from the adaptive playback system. Harmonic Atlas could make the route a composition and the surrounding graph its adaptive possibility space.

For the MVP, I would protect five things: generated harmonic maps, tap-to-play, route recording, two stackable layers, and one branching route that reliably converges. That is enough to demonstrate the unique idea without building a DAW or full game-audio middleware suite.
