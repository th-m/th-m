# Writing Hacks

Make prose easier to follow and remember by controlling the reader's path
through sentence topics, information order, imagery, and expectations. Apply
these techniques as diagnoses rather than universal style rules.

## Set the Pass

Identify the text, intended reader, and whether the user wants evaluation or
revision. Read the complete selection once before changing it. Record where the
reader loses the subject, encounters unprepared information, meets an abstraction
with nothing to picture, or receives unnecessary narration about the text.

An evaluation is read-only. Edit prose or files only when the user explicitly
asks for revision or implementation.

Use this skill when the argument and broad structure already exist. When local
friction is caused by a missing thesis, unsupported inference, or confused
section order, identify that upstream constraint instead of polishing around it.

## 1. Align the Two Skis

Track two levels at once:

- **Sentence topic:** the person, object, or concept each sentence makes the
  grammatical subject and asks the reader to follow.
- **Passage context:** the larger question or claim the paragraph, section, or
  chapter is developing.

List the grammatical subjects across the passage. Classify each as an anchor
that carries the context, a bridge that advances it, or a drift that starts an
unprepared subject chain. Prefer short, concrete, familiar subjects when the
meaning permits. Repeat an important topic when repetition makes the global
meaning visible; synonym variety is not a clarity goal.

Recommend a subject change only when it improves the relationship between the
sentence and its passage. Do not erase a necessary perspective shift.

## 2. Build Old-to-New Flow

Treat adjacent sentences as a relay:

1. Identify information already available to the reader.
2. Place that old or familiar information near the beginning of the next
   sentence when it can serve as the handoff.
3. Place the sentence's new, surprising, or consequential information near the
   end, where emphasis naturally accumulates.
4. Test whether that new information becomes a useful handoff for what follows.

Mark a flow break when a sentence opens with an unprepared concept or buries its
main contribution before the end. Passive voice is available when it preserves
a known topic in the subject or opening position; choose it for continuity, not
by default.

## 3. Give Memory Something to Hold

Locate abstract claims, nominalizations, and general categories that carry
important meaning without supplying a scene, action, or example. Where useful:

- ground an abstraction in a concrete example;
- name a human actor and an observable action;
- add relevant visual, auditory, tactile, spatial, or emotional detail;
- let an abstract concept act as a grammatical subject when the personification
  clarifies a real relationship.

Vividness must remain accurate. Never invent an anecdote, sensory detail, person,
or causal action. Preserve technical terms when precision matters, then make
their consequences perceivable around them.

## 4. Manage Expectations Without Empty Metadiscourse

Find phrases that merely announce the writing, such as “this section discusses”
or “the next paragraph will show.” Determine whether each phrase performs real
navigation work.

For redundant signposting, move directly into one of these:

- the reader's next genuine question;
- the claim that answers it;
- a transition expressed as shared movement through the subject, such as
  arriving at a problem or turning from causes to consequences.

Retain explicit signposts when they help readers navigate long technical,
procedural, legal, or accessibility-sensitive material. The target is earned
orientation, not the elimination of every sentence that names the document.

## Classify and Prioritize

Use three states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Aligned | The technique supports the reader without drawing attention to itself. |
| Friction | The reader can recover the path, but the prose imposes avoidable work. |
| Break | The topic, handoff, image, or expectation no longer supports reliable understanding. |

Prioritize topic-context alignment before old-to-new flow, then vividness and
expectation management. A weak topic chain often makes every later sentence
feel less connected.

## Deliver the Result

For an evaluation, return:

1. **Reader-path verdict:** where the passage flows and its main source of
   friction.
2. **Four-pass diagnostic:** status, exact evidence, reader effect, and smallest
   useful move for each technique.
3. **Topic chain:** the important grammatical subjects in order, with drifts
   marked.
4. **Handoff examples:** up to three old-to-new transitions that work or break.
5. **Revision priorities:** no more than three changes ordered by reader impact.

Use short quotations or stable section and line references. Include strengths;
the purpose is to preserve mechanisms that already work, not only flag defects.

When revision is requested, make the smallest coherent changes that repair the
priorities. Preserve meaning, factual scope, citations, terminology, and voice.
Re-read the revised passage in sequence and report which reader-path problems
were resolved and which tradeoffs remain.
