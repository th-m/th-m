# Clarity Techniques

Diagnose clarity at three linked levels: sentences show characters performing
actions, paragraphs create a coherent topic and information path, and the whole
piece gives readers a problem-shaped reason to follow its idea.

## Set the Review Boundary

Identify the text, intended reader, purpose, and whether the user wants an
evaluation or an implemented revision. Read the complete selection once before
analyzing local sentences. Record the point a reader can recover and where that
point becomes difficult to follow.

Read [the nine-technique catalog](references/nine-technique-catalog.md) before
evaluating. Use the complete framework for an article or essay. For a focused
request, evaluate the named level and any dependency needed to explain it.

An evaluation is read-only. Change prose or files only when the user explicitly
asks for revision or implementation.

## Build Three Maps

Create only the maps relevant to the supplied text:

1. **Character-action map:** identify the important conceptual or human
   characters, their grammatical positions, their main actions, and actions
   hidden in nouns.
2. **Paragraph path:** list sentence subjects and the old-to-new handoffs between
   adjacent sentences. Mark where topics scatter or complexity arrives before
   the reader has a foothold.
3. **Idea shape:** identify the motivating problem, the thesis or proposed
   solution, section boundaries, repeated key concepts, and the index and
   discussion of each major unit.

Use only evidence available in the text. Do not supply missing actors, actions,
problems, or stakes from background knowledge.

## Evaluate All Three Levels

Assess the nine techniques in their catalog order, but diagnose dependencies
from idea to paragraph to sentence. A sentence-level symptom may be caused by an
unresolved point or section purpose.

Classify each technique:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Clear | The text gives the reader a reliable character, action, path, or idea shape. |
| Strained | The relationship is recoverable but imposes avoidable work. |
| Broken | The relationship is missing, contradictory, or too displaced to recover reliably. |
| N/O | The supplied text does not expose enough of this level to judge. |

Support each material judgment with a short quotation or stable section and
line reference. Name the reader-facing consequence rather than merely naming a
construction such as passive voice or nominalization.

## Preserve Useful Exceptions

The techniques are design heuristics, not mechanical bans:

- Keep passive voice when it preserves the established topic, foregrounds the
  affected entity, or appropriately leaves the actor unknown.
- Keep nominalizations when they link back to prior meaning, replace an awkward
  clause, serve as a concrete object, or name a familiar domain concept.
- Keep point-last or delayed structures when they intentionally create discovery
  and still provide enough indexing for the reader.
- Keep complex openings when their order is the meaning, not merely the writer's
  first formulation.

Clarity must preserve factual scope, technical precision, uncertainty, and
voice. Do not turn every sentence into the same subject-verb pattern.

## Deliver the Evaluation

Return:

1. **Clarity verdict:** the idea a reader can currently recover, the strongest
   level, and the principal break across levels.
2. **Nine-technique matrix:** state, textual evidence, reader effect, and
   smallest useful move for every observable technique.
3. **Character-action findings:** the important subjects, displaced characters,
   active verbs, and consequential nominalizations.
4. **Paragraph findings:** topic chains, broken handoffs, and misplaced
   complexity.
5. **Idea findings:** problem framing, coherence signals, and index-discussion
   shape.
6. **Revision order:** no more than three high-leverage repairs, ordered from
   idea to paragraph to sentence unless a local edit is truly independent.

Describe mechanisms that already work so revision preserves them. Separate
clarity from factual accuracy and flag claims needing verification without
fact-checking them unless requested.

## Revise When Requested

Start with the highest-level constraint that explains the most friction. Make
the smallest coherent change, then re-read the affected unit at all three
levels. Report which states improved, what remained intentionally unchanged,
and any tradeoff introduced.
