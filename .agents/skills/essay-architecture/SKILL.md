# Essay Architecture

Diagnose an essay as a designed reading experience. Treat the framework's
patterns as recurring questions and tradeoffs, not as rules, templates, or a
license to flatten the author's voice.

## Establish the Review Contract

1. Identify the essay, intended audience, purpose, and draft stage from the
   request and available context.
2. Default to a full 27-pattern assessment for a complete draft. Limit the
   review to named dimensions, elements, or patterns when the user requests a
   focused assessment.
3. Read the complete essay once before scoring. Preserve the first-pass reader
   experience: where attention rises, stalls, becomes confused, or resolves.
4. Read [the framework catalog](references/framework.md) before evaluating.
5. If the essay lives in the repository, use stable section names and line
   references where practical. Do not modify the source during an evaluation.

An evaluation request is read-only. Rewrite prose or edit files only when the
user explicitly asks for revision or implementation.

## Build the Evidence Map

Before assigning scores, record:

- the apparent thesis in one sentence;
- the promise made by the title and opening;
- the function of each section or paragraph;
- the central tension and how it develops;
- the dominant voice, tone shifts, and recurring images or phrases;
- claims that require factual or source verification outside this framework.

Distinguish textual evidence from interpretation. Cite a short excerpt or a
precise location for every material judgment. Never infer factual truth,
originality, or authorial intent from rhetorical strength alone.

## Score Consistently

Use this operational rubric. It is a local diagnostic aid, not a claim about
Michael Dean's proprietary scoring guidance.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Absent or actively working against the essay's purpose. |
| 2 | Present but weak, confusing, generic, or inconsistent. |
| 3 | Functional and clear, with meaningful room to improve. |
| 4 | Strong, deliberate, and effective for this essay. |
| 5 | Exceptional and mutually reinforcing with other patterns. |
| N/O | Not observable from the supplied text; explain what is missing. |

Score relative to the essay's purpose, audience, genre, and stage. Do not punish
an intentional omission merely because a pattern could appear. A low score must
name the reader-facing consequence; a high score must name the mechanism that
works. Avoid false precision: dimension averages may summarize the review, but
they do not replace the evidence or rank unlike essays objectively.

## Evaluate in Dependency Order

1. **Idea:** Test whether the material supports a clear, consequential thesis
   and whether the title makes an honest, compelling promise.
2. **Form:** Test how paragraphs, structure, and tension stage that idea for a
   reader. Diagnose form problems as downstream when the idea is unresolved.
3. **Voice:** Test how spirit, sound, and sight make the argument feel authored,
   memorable, and embodied without obscuring meaning.
4. Revisit interaction effects. Identify cases where one pattern strengthens or
   weakens several others, such as a vague thesis causing weak cohesion and an
   inert finale.

Do not turn the catalog into 27 isolated copyediting comments. Prefer the few
architectural constraints that explain many local symptoms.

## Deliver the Assessment

For a full review, return these sections in order:

1. **Verdict:** two to four sentences describing the essay's current promise,
   strongest dimension, and principal constraint.
2. **Dimension summary:** Idea, Form, and Voice scores with one-sentence
   rationales. If useful, calculate each from observable pattern scores only.
3. **Pattern matrix:** all requested patterns with score, evidence, diagnosis,
   and reader effect. Keep each cell concise.
4. **Strengths:** the three most effective architectural choices and why they
   work.
5. **Revision priorities:** no more than three high-leverage changes, ordered by
   dependency. For each, name the affected patterns, proposed move, intended
   effect, and any tradeoff.
6. **Verification boundary:** list factual, sourcing, or audience assumptions
   the prose alone cannot establish.

For a focused review, keep the same evidence standard but include only the
requested scope and the cross-pattern dependencies needed to explain it.

## Revise Without Homogenizing

When revision is explicitly requested:

1. Agree on or infer the highest-leverage priority from the assessment.
2. Preserve deliberate voice, technical meaning, citations, and content
   contracts.
3. Make the smallest coherent revision that tests the diagnosis.
4. Re-read the affected section in context and rescore only the patterns the
   change was intended to improve.
5. Report what changed, what remained intentionally unresolved, and any new
   tradeoff introduced.

Do not optimize every pattern to a five. Essays need hierarchy, variation, and
intentional restraint.
