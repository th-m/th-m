<Section index="01" title="What is a conspiracy?">

Conspiracy is defined as a [secret agreement to carry out a wrongful scheme](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/conspiracy).

I will show why this is not a constructive lens for evaluating complex systems.
It invites appeals to authority: “It wasn't secret because our trusted president
was in the meeting.” It requires proof of individual knowledge: “You cannot prove
Monsanto knew that pesticides harmful to crops and animals could also harm humans.”
Lastly, it requires knowing individual intent: “If COVID leaked from a Wuhan lab,
was it intentional?” The discussion gets stuck on intent and never reaches the
consequential outcomes.

There is also a kind of semantic paradox: if “secret” is treated as meaning
“still hidden,” a conspiracy seems to stop fitting the definition as soon as
it is proven and exposed. This enables the fallacious argument: “That's not a
secret, and therefore not a conspiracy.”

</Section>

Recurring sales, restricted competition, favorable research, and protection
from accountability offer financial advantages. Deliberate coordination,
ordinary incentives, opportunism, and institutional failures can produce

govern conscience and belief?
