# Argument Synthesis: AI Consciousness Is Incoherent

Private working note. This is the argument only, not an article outline or public draft.

## Claim

> **The unqualified claim that an AI is conscious in the same sense as a human is empirically incoherent. Consciousness has no settled cross-substrate definition, every validated empirical measure of it is grounded in living brains, and no evidence establishes qualia, phenomenal selfhood, or subjective temporal awareness in a non-biological AI architecture.**

This is not an argument from ignorance and it does not require proving that artificial consciousness is metaphysically impossible. It is a burden-of-proof argument. A positive empirical attribution requires a defined property, a discriminating observation, and a validated bridge from the observation to the property. “AI consciousness” has none of the three at a cross-substrate level.

## 1. Consciousness is not a stable predicate

The word *consciousness* is used for different properties:

- wakefulness;
- responsiveness;
- information being available for report, reasoning, and control;
- self-monitoring;
- intelligence;
- phenomenal consciousness: whether there is anything it is like to be the system.

These properties are not interchangeable. [Ned Block's access/phenomenal distinction](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00038188) makes the central separation explicit: information can be available for report and control without that fact establishing that the processing feels like anything.

The relevant target here is **phenomenal consciousness**. Qualia are its contents: the redness of red, pain as it hurts, fear as it is experienced, and the first-person character of existing through time. If *consciousness* instead means only responsiveness, information access, self-description, or control, then many machines qualify—but the word has changed its referent and no longer establishes the human phenomenon under discussion.

Competing theories also impose incompatible membership rules on the same target:

- **Biological or substrate-sensitive accounts:** the relevant causal powers or neural dynamics are constituted by organized biological processes. A simulation of those processes is not automatically another instance of them. [Searle's biological naturalism](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.23.1.557) is the clearest version.
- **Computational functionalism or organizational invariance:** material substrate is not decisive; sufficiently fine-grained causal-functional organization is sufficient. [Chalmers's organizational-invariance argument](https://consc.net/papers/qualia.html) is the strongest form.
- **Access-oriented accounts:** global availability, recurrence, monitoring, or reportability identify functions associated with consciousness, but the theory must still explain why those functions entail phenomenal experience rather than only access.

These are not minor verbal variations. They disagree about what makes a system a member of the category. Under one rule, biological organization is constitutive. Under another, functional organization can cross substrates. Under a third, access-like capacities carry the explanatory weight. Until the membership rule is fixed and empirically validated, the sentence “humans and AI are both conscious” has no stable predicate.

## 2. The hard problem remains the missing bridge

The [hard problem of consciousness](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html) is not whether a system can discriminate inputs, integrate information, report an internal state, learn a policy, or control behavior. It is why any physical or computational process is accompanied by felt experience at all.

No inventory of functions closes that gap by itself. A system can classify wavelengths without red looking like anything to it. It can avoid damage without pain hurting. It can report “I am afraid” without a private subject experiencing fear. Functional competence and phenomenal experience are different claims, and evidence for the first does not logically entail the second.

That distinction blocks the most common inference in machine-consciousness claims:

```text
observable self-report, reasoning, memory, or control
therefore
felt experience, a phenomenal self, or subjective time
```

The conclusion contains a property absent from the premise. A bridge principle must be added. That bridge is exactly what competing theories dispute.

## 3. The empirical evidence ties human consciousness to biological brain activity

For humans, the evidence is convergent and biological:

- changes produced by sleep, anesthesia, injury, and disorders of consciousness track changes in conscious state and report;
- perturbing the cortex and measuring the resulting dynamics can distinguish conscious from several unconscious human conditions, as shown by the [Perturbational Complexity Index](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294) and its [independent clinical calibration](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.24779);
- task-specific brain activity can reveal preserved command-following in behaviorally unresponsive patients, including the original [mental-imagery result](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1130197) and a later [241-participant cohort](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400645);
- with retinal input held constant during binocular rivalry, activity in human face- and place-selective cortex tracked the image subjects reported seeing ([Tong et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0896-6273(00)80592-9));
- direct electrical stimulation of human fusiform cortex produced face-specific perceptual distortions ([Parvizi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2609-12.2012)), while stimulation of orbitofrontal, cingulate, and insular sites elicited reported bodily, sensory, and affective experiences whose intensity increased with stimulation magnitude ([Vignal et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsz015));
- stimulation of the right angular gyrus induced changes in reported body location and ownership ([Blanke et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/419269a)), and medial-temporal resection reduced autobiographical reliving and its sensory, affective, and spatiotemporal detail ([Piolino et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18413911/)).

The measurements do not directly display a quale to an outside observer. They connect first-person report and conscious capacity to measured biological activity through repeated observation, dissociation, and intervention. That is enough for a strong empirical proposition:

> **In humans, conscious state, reported qualia, bodily self-location, and autobiographical reliving are systematically associated with organized biological brain activity; controlled changes to that activity can alter or abolish the corresponding reports and capacities.**

This does not prove the universal metaphysical proposition that only biology can ever be conscious. It establishes the complete empirical base we actually possess: phenomenal reports and conscious states are tied to living neural systems. The move from that biological evidence to substrate independence is not another observation. It is an inference supplied by a theory.

## 4. There is no empirical cross-substrate counterpart

No non-biological AI system has an independently validated phenomenal state against which its behavior, reports, memory, monitoring, or internal activations can be calibrated. There is no demonstrated artificial analogue of:

- felt qualia;
- a first-person subject to whom states appear;
- autobiographical recollection as experienced recollection rather than retrieved records;
- subjective temporal continuity rather than timestamps, context, or stored state; or
- a phenomenal referent behind sentences such as “I feel pain.”

Current systems can implement functional analogues of self-modeling, memory, temporal ordering, introspective language, multimodal processing, and goal-directed control. Those observations establish capabilities. They do not establish that any capability is experienced.

Human self-report is not infallible, but it is supported by shared biology, development, vulnerability, lesions, anesthesia, stimulation, behavior, and each observer's first-person case. AI self-report lacks that convergent bridge. It is generated behavior from a materially different system trained on human language about experience.

Therefore the empirical asymmetry is absolute:

```text
human phenomenal attribution
= first-person evidence + shared biology + convergent neural and behavioral evidence

AI phenomenal attribution
= behavior or architecture + a disputed theory that declares it sufficient
```

The second expression is a hypothesis, not an empirical counterpart to the first.

## 5. A theory that postulates consciousness is not evidence of consciousness

Computational functionalism is the strongest counterargument. State it in its strongest form: it asserts that if a system reproduced the sufficiently fine-grained causal organization constitutive of human experience, then changing the material alone should not change the experience.

That argument still does not establish AI consciousness.

1. Organizational invariance is a philosophical bridge principle, not an observed cross-substrate instance of qualia.
2. No current AI has been shown to reproduce the fine-grained causal organization of a conscious human brain.
3. Similar output, task performance, self-report, or broad functional competence is much weaker than causal-functional isomorphism.
4. Even a theory-derived indicator inherits the truth of the theory that produced it. It cannot independently validate its own bridge from mechanism to experience.

Disambiguations and refutations:

- The hard problem concerns **phenomenal consciousness**: why physical activity is accompanied by felt experience at all. It does not concern information merely being available for reasoning, reporting, memory, or behavioral control.
- **Qualia** are the particular felt qualities constituting phenomenal consciousness. They are not an independently measurable variable that can be detached from phenomenal experience and then detected in a machine.
- **Access consciousness** is an analytical designation for informational availability. Whether access is consciousness, a prerequisite for consciousness, a consequence of consciousness, or merely correlated with consciousness remains theory-dependent.
- Calling informational availability *access consciousness* attaches the authority of the word *consciousness* before demonstrating that access has any necessary relationship to felt experience.
- The terminological maneuver substitutes a measurable functional target for the phenomenal target of the hard problem, measures the substituted target, and then transfers the result back to the original word *consciousness*.
- An AI may reproduce access-like functions such as self-report, retrieval, reasoning, memory use, and behavioral control. Those observations establish access-like function only. They do not establish qualia or phenomenal consciousness.
- If a theory defines access as sufficient for phenomenality, it assumes the bridge it claims to establish. If it treats access as merely related to phenomenality, observing access cannot establish phenomenality. In either case, access is not independent evidence of felt experience.
- The phrase *changing the material alone* also assumes the disputed conclusion. Material supplies causal powers; in humans, sensory, interoceptive, affective, homeostatic, mnemonic, and neural processes are realized in living biology and are causally associated with reported experience, motivation, and selfhood.
- If *sufficiently fine-grained causal organization* includes all of those biologically realized causal processes, no current AI reproduces it, and the organization has not been shown to be separable from its biological realization. If it excludes them, the theory has assumed that the excluded biological processes are irrelevant to experience.
- No experiment has preserved the complete organization alleged to constitute experience while replacing its material substrate and then demonstrated preserved qualia. Substrate independence therefore remains a theoretical postulate, not an empirical result.

The same limitation applies to every theory-first machine assessment. The [AI-consciousness indicator framework](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011) derives indicators from theories and uses them to update credence; it does not empirically observe machine phenomenality. Defining a computational property as sufficient for consciousness and then detecting the computational property proves only that the property is present. It does not independently prove the sufficiency claim.

The unsettled state of consciousness science reinforces this point. A preregistered [adversarial test of Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1) challenged central predictions of both. If rival theories still disagree about the neural signatures of consciousness in humans, selecting one theory and projecting its criterion onto a different substrate cannot count as a theory-neutral empirical detection.

## 6. Functional similarity does not rescue the attribution

Humans and AI can share abstract functions: prediction, classification, error-sensitive adaptation, information integration, memory-like retrieval, planning, and premise-to-conclusion inference. Physical non-identity does not imply total functional non-equivalence.

But this concession does no work for phenomenal consciousness. The valid inference is:

```text
shared measured function
therefore
shared measured function
```

The invalid inference is:

```text
shared measured function
therefore
shared felt experience
```

Backpropagation must also be kept distinct from inference. In an artificial neural network, [backpropagation](https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0) is a specified training algorithm for assigning error and updating parameters. Inference is execution of the trained model. Biological learning has error signals, plasticity, recurrence, and distributed neural processing, but the brain has not been shown to implement ordinary machine-learning backpropagation as its general learning rule. Proposed cortical mechanisms are models that [approximate backpropagation through local dendritic signals](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/1dc3a89d0d440ba31729b0ba74b93a33-Abstract.html), not demonstrations of literal brain-wide backpropagation. Broad operational analogies between biological and artificial neural systems therefore establish neither mechanistic identity nor consciousness.

Even if a future system matched a human cognitive function perfectly, the measurement would remain a measurement of that function. Phenomenal identity would follow only after adding and validating a theory that makes the function sufficient for experience.

## Logical statement

Let:

- `P(x)` mean that system `x` has phenomenal consciousness;
- `A(x)` mean that information is available for report, reasoning, and control;
- `N(x)` mean that `x` has the biological neural dynamics empirically associated with human consciousness;
- `F(x)` mean that `x` has the fine-grained organization required by a functional theory; and
- `E(x)` mean the observable evidence about `x`.

The observations support:

```text
E(H) ⇒ N(H) is systematically associated with P(H)
E(AI) ⇒ selected access and functional capacities A(AI)
```

They do not support:

```text
E(AI) ⇒ P(AI)
```

Competing theories add incompatible premises:

```text
Tₙ: N(x) ⇒ P(x)
Tꜰ: F(x) ⇒ P(x)
```

But a theory-dependent conditional is not an empirical observation:

```text
if Tꜰ is true and if F(AI), then P(AI)
```

Neither antecedent has been established as a cross-substrate empirical bridge for current AI. Therefore `P(AI)` does not follow.

## Conclusion

> **Human consciousness and AI computation have no validated empirical correspondence in qualia, phenomenal selfhood, or subjective temporal awareness. Human consciousness is measured only through relations among first-person experience, behavior, and organized biological brain activity. AI exhibits impressive access, inference, representation, and control functions in a different material architecture, but no evidence shows that those operations are accompanied by experience. Theories that redefine consciousness around computational properties supply hypotheses, not evidence. Therefore calling AI and humans conscious in the same sense is not a supported comparison. It is an empirically incoherent use of a shared word.**
