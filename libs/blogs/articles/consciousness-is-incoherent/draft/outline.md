# AI's Consciousness explanation

## Thesis

The essay does not claim that artificial consciousness is impossible. It
claims that an unqualified cross-substrate use of *consciousness* is
underdetermined. Machine-consciousness claims become coherent only after they
name a theory, a discriminating measurement, and a validated bridge from the
measured mechanism to phenomenal experience.

## Rough-copy threads retained

- Open with “Is an AI conscious? Depends on definition.”
- Define the target through qualia: redness, sourdough, fear, and pain.
- Separate the strong human neural evidence from the unsupported inference
  that only biology could ever produce consciousness.
- Use “play with fire and get burned” to distinguish a felt learning history
  from a functionally similar avoidance policy.
- Contrast biological accounts with computational functionalism.
- Explain why “humans and AI are both conscious” is too coarse to be
  explanatory, much like an unqualified comparison based on determinism.
- End with the need for novel, falsifiable probes.

## Logical spine

For artificial system `A`, evidence `E`, access capacity `Access`, and
phenomenal consciousness `P`:

```text
E(A) ⇒ Access(A)
Tꜰ: F(A) ⇒ P(A)
Tɴ: N(A) ⇒ P(A)
E(A) ⇏ P(A)
```

The theory-specific expressions supply incompatible bridge principles. The
last rejects the move from behavior, computation, or self-report alone to an
unqualified phenomenal attribution.

## Guardrails

- Do not say that neuroscience has no objective consciousness measures.
- Do say that there is no accepted, theory-independent cross-substrate measure
  of phenomenal consciousness.
- Do not infer that biological dependence in humans proves biological
  necessity everywhere.
- Do not infer experience from self-report or task performance alone.
- Treat artificial consciousness as a live theoretical possibility and a
  currently unsupported attribution.
