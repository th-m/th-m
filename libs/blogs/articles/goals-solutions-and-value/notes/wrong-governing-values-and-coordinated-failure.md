# Wrong Governing Values and Coordinated Failure

A company with the wrong governing values may not become disorganized. It may
become **highly organized around the wrong objective**.

> **The company becomes coherently wrong.**

Values determine what receives funding, what gets measured, who gets promoted,
which complaints are ignored, and what counts as success. Once translated into
incentives and procedures, they shape thousands of local decisions without the
leader making each one personally.

```text
Governing values
→ metrics and incentives
→ repeated employee decisions
→ customer and employee consequences
→ organizational data
→ reinforcement of the governing values
```

For example:

| Governing priority | Likely behavior | Possible consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth above trust | Dark patterns and aggressive acquisition | Churn, regulation, brand erosion |
| Speed above reliability | Ship without adequate validation | Outages and accumulated technical debt |
| Efficiency above learning | Remove slack and experimentation | Short-term savings, long-term stagnation |
| Harmony above truth | Punish disagreement | Leaders lose access to corrective evidence |
| Metrics above purpose | Optimize visible indicators | Employees game measurements |
| Control above autonomy | Centralize every decision | Slow adaptation and loss of capable people |
| Revenue above customer welfare | Extract rather than create value | Customers leave when alternatives appear |

## False Evaluative Closure

This creates an important extension of the evaluative-closure argument.

A system has evaluative closure when it possesses criteria and feedback for
determining whether an action is better. But closure is not necessarily
correct.

> **If the governing values are wrong, evaluative closure becomes a trap: every
> component can perform correctly while the organization moves consistently in
> the wrong direction.**

The tests pass because the tests embody the wrong priorities. Dashboards remain
green because the dashboards omit the important consequences. Employees
receive rewards for behavior that damages the company's actual long-term
viability.

The organization can therefore optimize itself into failure.

## Why the Problem Compounds

Wrong values alter the feedback available to leadership:

1. Employees learn which information leaders reward.
2. Bad news becomes personally costly to report.
3. Metrics replace the underlying human outcomes.
4. People harmed by the strategy leave or disengage.
5. Their absence is interpreted as confirmation rather than evidence.
6. The remaining organization becomes increasingly aligned with the original
   hierarchy.

This produces a self-confirming system. The company does not merely make bad
decisions; it gradually loses the people and information capable of showing
why they are bad.

## AI Can Accelerate This Process

An AI system can make the company exceptionally consistent at applying the
hierarchy:

- automate decisions;
- enforce policies;
- rank employees or customers;
- allocate resources;
- filter feedback;
- optimize selected metrics; and
- reproduce the same priorities at scale.

If the hierarchy is wrong, better AI performance can make the outcome worse.
Instrumental competence increases the speed and reach of the mistake.

The AI might identify contradictions or predict harmful consequences, but it
cannot overrule the governing hierarchy unless it has been authorized to
challenge, escalate, or stop decisions.

## What Makes a Value Hierarchy Corrigible

A resilient company must treat its governing values as subject to evidence
rather than as untouchable slogans. It needs:

- independent feedback channels;
- protected dissent;
- direct customer observation;
- measurements of downstream consequences;
- multiple stakeholder perspectives;
- explicit tradeoff reviews;
- leading and lagging indicators;
- escalation paths outside the normal hierarchy; and
- authority to revise goals when evidence changes.

## Conclusion

> **A company fails not only when it cannot execute its values, but when it
> executes the wrong values so consistently that it suppresses the evidence
> needed to revise them.**

Or more compactly:

> **Operational alignment is powerful, but alignment with the wrong value
> hierarchy is coordinated failure.**
