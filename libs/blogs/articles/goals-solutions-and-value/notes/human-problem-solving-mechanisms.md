# Human Problem-Solving Mechanisms

## Mechanisms in the Loop

1. **Goal and valuation**
   Establishes what counts as success, which consequences matter, and how effort, risk, and reward should be traded off. Decision research decomposes this into representation, valuation, selection, outcome evaluation, and learning. [Rangel, Camerer, and Montague](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn2357)

2. **Problem framing**
   Constructs a representation of the current state, desired state, constraints, and relevant unknowns. A bad frame can make every subsequent inference irrelevant while remaining internally coherent.

3. **Attention and working memory**
   Selects relevant information, maintains intermediate results, and suppresses competing material. Attention, working memory, and executive control are overlapping but partially distinguishable systems. [Toba et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36967445/)

4. **Memory retrieval**
   Supplies semantic knowledge, remembered experiences, learned procedures, analogies, and prior strategies. Retrieval is reconstructive and shaped by the present goal rather than being a literal database lookup.

5. **Generation and simulation**
   Recombines memory into possible explanations, actions, and futures. The hippocampus and broader episodic network contribute to accessing and recombining details, although their exact roles remain unsettled. [Addis and Schacter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22291625/)

6. **Inference and relational integration**
   Derives conclusions by identifying relations, applying rules, using analogy, or estimating probable causes. Mental-model theory proposes that people construct possible situations and test what follows from them. [Johnson-Laird](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2972923/) Relational reasoning recruits distributed frontoparietal systems rather than one reasoning center. [Krawczyk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21129363/)

7. **Evaluation and selection**
   Compares candidates against goals, evidence, constraints, expected consequences, and effort. This is where a plausible idea should become a hypothesis to test rather than an explanation to defend.

8. **Performance monitoring**
   Detects conflict, surprise, errors, and differences between expected and actual outcomes. These signals can trigger more attention, a different strategy, or learning. [Ullsperger, Danielmeier, and Jocham](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00041.2012)

9. **Strategy control**
   Chooses which cognitive operation to perform next: retrieve, decompose, calculate, simulate, seek evidence, ask someone, or act. Researchers describe this as a cognitive “outer loop” that sequences lower-level operations. [Herd et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3722785/)

10. **Action and learning**
    Acting on the world provides evidence unavailable through additional internal reasoning. The result can update the problem frame, confidence, memory, or future strategy.
