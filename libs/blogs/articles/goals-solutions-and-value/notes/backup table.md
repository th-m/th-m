
### Do not overclaim the science

This table compares a human with only the trained LLM operating on its active
input context. The LLM column excludes tool calls, retrieval systems, saved
memory, agent controllers, and other external scaffolding. The LLM does not
have separate modules for knowledge, imagination, and reasoning. These
capabilities largely arise from the same transformer inference process, with
different inputs, learned representations, runtime state, and decoding paths.

| Capability | What it represents | Human | LLM mechanism | Comparative utility and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Modality** | Different modes through which information enters | People receive visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory, vestibular, proprioceptive, interoceptive, and other biological signals. Sensory transduction converts physical and bodily events into neural activity that can be integrated with attention, memory, emotion, and action. | Text tokenizers or modality encoders convert text, images, audio, or video into numerical embeddings. Projection layers align those embeddings with the transformer's token space, where attention can operate across them. Preprocessing may run on CPUs, while encoder and transformer tensor operations generally run on accelerator hardware such as GPUs or TPUs. | Humans normally associate some sensory processing with felt experience. Input diversity alone does not establish qualia, and there is no validated evidence that LLMs have it. |
| **Learning** | Information taken in and incorporated so that it can affect later behavior | Incoming information can produce episodic, statistical, reinforcement, procedural, perceptual, emotional, and social learning. Attention, prior knowledge, goals, practice, feedback, neural plasticity, and consolidation determine what produces lasting change. | A training objective—commonly next-token prediction during pretraining—produces a numerical loss. Backpropagation computes gradients, and an optimizer updates parameter tensors throughout the transformer. Distributed training executes these operations across GPUs or TPUs and stores weights, gradients, and optimizer state in accelerator memory. | Information entering a context is not durable learning. Once that context is absent, a base LLM does not retain the episode. Human learning is also selective, biased, and sometimes maladaptive. |
| **Memory** | Information retained and made available later | Working, episodic, semantic, and procedural systems preserve different consequences of experience through encoding, consolidation, retrieval, and reconstruction. Past experience can alter later perception, emotion, reasoning, and action. | Long-term statistical structure is encoded in parameter tensors produced by training. During inference, context tokens, intermediate activations, and an attention key-value cache maintain temporary sequence state in accelerator memory. The runtime state disappears when it is discarded. | Parameterized information is not autobiographical recollection. Persistent conversation history, databases, and retrieval belong to a larger system, not to the isolated LLM considered here. |
| **Imagination** | Possibilities generated without immediate observation | Mental imagery, episodic future simulation, counterfactual thinking, and recombination of memory allow people to construct absent or novel possibilities. These simulations can influence emotion, expectations, strategy, and later learning. | Transformer forward passes calculate a probability distribution over the next token. A decoding algorithm—such as greedy selection or probabilistic sampling—chooses a token, appends it to the sequence, and repeats. The process recombines patterns distributed across the model's parameters and current context. | This is a substantial functional generative capability. It does not by itself establish phenomenal experience, personal significance, or a persistent inner model changed by the generated scenario. |
| **Knowledge** | Information organized into representations that can be used | Human knowledge combines semantic and episodic memory, practiced skills, perception, testimony, culture, causal models, and action. Present context and goals control which relationships are retrieved and applied. | Optimization during training distributes statistical, linguistic, conceptual, and relational structure across token embeddings, attention projections, feed-forward layers, and other parameter tensors. At runtime, those tensors are loaded from storage into accelerator memory and applied to the active input. | Neither biological nor artificial representation guarantees truth. An isolated LLM cannot inspect current external evidence unless that evidence is supplied in its input, and it does not contain all digital information or compress it into one vector embedding. |
| **Reasoning** | Conclusions, explanations, and plans formed, tested, or revised | Humans use deduction, induction, abduction, analogy, causal reasoning, counterfactual thought, and strategy. These forms draw on overlapping working-memory, cognitive-control, memory, and domain-specific networks. | Each generated token results from transformer layers repeatedly applying self-attention, feed-forward transformations, normalization, and residual connections to the sequence state. Autoregressive decoding feeds generated tokens back into subsequent forward passes, allowing intermediate text or hidden representations to condition later computation. | The same reasoning function can be implemented differently. The isolated LLM has no separate symbolic reasoning engine unless one is built into the model architecture. Humans and LLMs both make systematic errors. |
