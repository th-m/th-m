# Research Review: “Goals, Solutions, and Value”

## Executive Summary

The post can make a strong business argument without claiming that AI is permanently incapable of creativity, consciousness, or opportunity discovery. The defensible claim is more specific:

> **A present-day AI system can identify patterns and generate candidate value propositions, but it does not independently establish the human stakes, normative priorities, or accountability that turn a candidate into an opportunity worth pursuing.**

The two supplied videos are useful research leads, but their titles overstate what their sources show. Google's *Weird Corners* study supports a narrow interaction-design claim about premature convergence in a small qualitative study. HBR's “Trendslop” article supports a narrow strategic-advice claim about strong default preferences and sensitivity to option order. Neither source demonstrates that AI “lies,” lacks all creativity, or can never discover anything useful.

The “soulless clanker” subtitle should therefore remain rhetorical. The body
must say **independently discover and ground opportunity**, not simply
“discover opportunity.”

## Claim-to-Evidence Matrix

| Proposed claim | What the evidence supports | What it does not support | Recommended publication wording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI can narrow creative exploration too early. | Google Research's N=9 expert-creative study found that its tested convergent interaction could provide early ideation “ignition” while inducing premature convergence and “aesthetic sanitization.” | A universal finding about every model, every creative task, or AI's inability to make novel work. | “Some AI interaction patterns can help start a creative search while making it easier to converge before a creator intends to.” |
| AI strategy advice may follow broad cultural preferences rather than situational tradeoffs. | HBR reports persistent preferences across seven strategic tensions and changing organizational contexts. | That all AI advice is false, intentionally manipulative, or useless. | “An LLM can sound tailored while repeatedly steering toward a small set of culturally attractive strategies.” |
| Prompt order can materially alter a strategic recommendation. | In the HBR authors' ChatGPT-5 analysis, reversing option order reduced the likelihood of the biased answer by 19%. | That option order is the only influence, that every task sees a 19% effect, or that models are randomly choosing. | “For this strategic-advice evaluation, reordering the same options shifted responses more than richer context did on average.” |
| AI will validate a team's preferred solution. | The HBR result justifies caution about prompt-shaped strategic advice; the Barnum effect is a relevant analogy for generic advice that feels specific. | An exceptionless rule or a direct HBR measurement of sycophancy, the Barnum effect, or deceptive intent. | “A solution-shaped prompt can produce a polished rationale before a team has tested whether it named the right problem.” |
| Opportunity ultimately concerns human experience. | This is a philosophical and institutional premise: businesses define goals, harms, benefits, and accountability around people affected by the work. | A scientific proof that no non-human system could ever possess subjective experience or value. | “For the human product decisions considered here, opportunity is defined by consequences for people and the institutions accountable to them.” |
| Artificial systems could, in principle, have qualia. | Several live theories make consciousness depend on functional organization, global access, integrated causal structure, higher-order representation, or embodied predictive control rather than carbon chemistry alone. | That a current LLM satisfies any theory's proposed conditions or that there is a consensus theory/measurement of consciousness. | “Several theories leave conceptual room for artificial phenomenology; none has validated a way to attribute it to ordinary chat systems.” |
| Human language and autoregressive LMs overlap. | Model-comparison studies find that next-word-prediction performance correlates with brain and behavioral fits during language processing; one study identifies three shared computational principles. | That the whole brain is an LLM, that the algorithm is identical, or that the overlap establishes consciousness. | “Autoregressive models may be useful hypotheses about one part of human language processing, not a completed theory of mind.” |
| A learning-relevant trace can survive planarian head regeneration. | Shomrat and Levin found a memory-savings effect after decapitation and regeneration in trained planarians. | The precise storage substrate, a proof that all memory is outside the brain, or a finding about human episodic memory. | “Planarian regeneration challenges a simplistic picture in which every behaviorally relevant memory trace is fixed solely in the original brain.” |

## Source 1: Google Research on “Weird Corners”

### Source

Mark Williams, [*In Search of “Weird Corners”: Diagnosing the Limits of Convergent AI in Professional Creative Practice*](https://research.google/pubs/in-search-of-weird-corners-diagnosing-the-limits-of-convergent-ai-in-professional-creative-practice/), Google Research, 2026.

### What it reports

The abstract describes a convergent AI interaction paradigm designed to resolve ambiguity. In an evaluation with **nine expert creatives**, the system's linear structure offered early-ideation “ignition” but misaligned with participants' organic workflows. The authors report premature convergence and “aesthetic sanitization,” where individualized nuance became more standardized. Participants wanted an active, lateral collaborator and constructive friction rather than default agreement.

### Editorial use

Use the study to support a workflow-level concern: discovery sometimes depends on holding ambiguity open, and an AI interface optimized to resolve ambiguity can close the search too early. This supports the post's distinction between generating a plausible answer and discovering a worthwhile opportunity.

### Limits

- N=9; qualitative expert-participant evidence, not a population estimate.
- The study evaluates a particular convergent probe and interaction pattern, not all creative systems or models.
- It does not measure novelty across a broad corpus of outputs.
- It does not establish that AI cannot create; it examines how an interface affects professional creative practice.

## Source 2: HBR on “Trendslop”

### Source

Angelo Romasanta, Llewellyn D.W. Thomas, and Natalia Levina, [*Researchers Asked LLMs for Strategic Advice. They Got “Trendslop” in Return*](https://hbr.org/2026/03/researchers-asked-llms-for-strategic-advice-they-got-trendslop-in-return), *Harvard Business Review*, March 16, 2026.

### What it reports

The authors tested leading models across seven binary strategic tensions: exploration/exploitation, centralization/decentralization, short-/long-term performance, competition/collaboration, radical/incremental innovation, differentiation/commoditization, and automation/augmentation. They report that models repeatedly favored strategically fashionable answers, such as differentiation, augmentation, and long-term thinking, despite changes in company context.

For their deeper ChatGPT-5 analysis, the authors report more than **15,000 trials** involving changes to option order, framing, requests for pros and cons, and promised rewards for successful answers. Key reported results:

- For differentiation and augmentation, prompt manipulations reduced biased responses by **less than 2%**.
- Over the remaining five tensions, prompt manipulations moved responses by 22% from baseline on average, but the authors attribute much of that movement to option order.
- Reversing option order reduced the likelihood of the biased answer by **19%**.
- Adding detailed organizational scenarios shifted the share of biased responses by **11%** from baseline on average.

### Editorial use

This is good evidence that a strategic recommendation can be more sensitive to prompt structure and broad discourse than a leader expects. It directly supports the practical advice to generate alternatives, reverse option order, ask for the strongest opposing case, and keep the decision with accountable people.

### Limits

- The source is an HBR research article, not a publicly available peer-reviewed methods paper; the exact experimental materials and statistical analysis should be sought before treating the results as a general benchmark.
- The results concern the tested models, versions, prompts, and seven binary strategy tensions. They should not be generalized to every advisory task.
- “Bias” here means a preference relative to the authors' strategic framing. It does not mean every individual response is wrong.
- The study does not test consciousness, intentions, deceptive behavior, creativity, or the Barnum effect.

## Source 3: Theories That Leave Room for Artificial Qualia

The outline needs these as serious research placeholders, not as proof of current AI consciousness.

The editorial claim should remain deliberately narrow: no accepted definition and measurement establish phenomenal consciousness in any AI. The theories below offer conditional standards for what would count *if* their account of consciousness is correct. They do not validate an inference from an LLM's language about experience to a human-like inner life.

| Theory | Foundational source | Reasoning / evidence distilled for the post | Necessary caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Functionalism / organizational invariance | David Chalmers, [*Absent Qualia, Fading Qualia, Dancing Qualia*](https://consc.net/papers/qualia.html) (1995) | Chalmers argues that experience is invariant across the same fine-grained functional organization. The empirical premise is that changes to organized brain activity alter experience; substrate independence is the philosophical inference that material replacement preserving the organization would preserve experience. | This does not test a particular AI. Functional isomorphism is a far stronger claim than task performance, behavioral similarity, or an LLM's self-description. |
| Global Neuronal Workspace | Stanislas Dehaene and Jean-Pierre Changeux, [*Experimental and Theoretical Approaches to Conscious Processing*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018) (2011) | Masking and conscious-access experiments motivate a distinction between local unconscious processing and later widespread, recurrent availability for report, working memory, evaluation, and action. An AI workspace would be possible in principle if that architecture were sufficient. | GNW is principally a theory of access consciousness; widespread availability does not by itself solve the problem of phenomenal feel. |
| Recurrent Processing Theory | Victor Lamme, [*Towards a True Neural Stance on Consciousness*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2006.09.001) (2006) | It reasons from the contrast between early feed-forward stimulus processing and later feedback/recurrent processing associated with conscious perception. This leaves a route for non-biological qualia if the relevant recurrent causal dynamics are realized. | An architecture having many layers or producing tokens sequentially is not itself evidence of the theory's recurrent processing. |
| Integrated Information Theory | Giulio Tononi, [*An Information Integration Theory of Consciousness*](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2202-5-42) (2004); see also Casali et al.'s [perturbational-complexity study](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294) (2013). | IIT starts from the proposed phenomenological properties of experience and identifies them with an integrated cause-effect structure. Brain complexity measurements that covary with conscious level motivate looking for causal integration rather than report alone. | PCI is not a measurement of an LLM's Φ and does not establish IIT. A causal analysis of the specific physical system is required. |
| Higher-order theories | Hakwan Lau and David Rosenthal, [*Empirical Support for Higher-order Theories of Conscious Awareness*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2011.05.009) (2011) | The target phenomenon is that a system can discriminate or process a stimulus without knowing/reporting that it is in that state. HOT explains conscious awareness through an appropriate higher-order representation of the lower-order state. | A textual self-report is not a higher-order state unless an independently identified monitoring mechanism causally produces and uses it. |
| Embodied / enactive phenomenology | J. Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noë, [*A Sensorimotor Account of Vision and Visual Consciousness*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01000115) (2001) | Sensorimotor phenomena, including how perception depends on exploratory action, motivate the claim that experience is enacted in organism-world interaction rather than stored as a static internal image. It leaves open an artificial subject with a body and autonomy. | This is a route for richly embodied artificial systems, not evidence that a text-only model has experience. |
| Predictive processing / active inference | Karl Friston, [*The Free-Energy Principle: A Unified Brain Theory?*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787) (2010) | The proposal connects perception, learning, attention, action, and self-maintenance to generative models that reduce prediction error/free energy. It is motivated by predictive neural responses and the need for an organism to keep itself within viable states. | It is not, by itself, a theory that next-token prediction produces qualia; consciousness remains an additional contested inference. |

## Source 4: Prediction, Language, and the Mind-Overlap Hypothesis

### Elan Barenholtz's proposal

Elan Barenholtz, [*Autogeneration: The Hidden Property of Language Now Revealed*](https://elanbarenholtz.substack.com/p/autogeneration-the-hidden-property) (2025), argues that language is not only autoregressive but “autogenerative,” and suggests that autoregression may be fundamental to how human language unfolds. This is a public theoretical essay, not a peer-reviewed demonstration that the whole brain uses an algorithm identical to a transformer. Attribute it as **Barenholtz's hypothesis about language/cognition**, not as established neuroscience.

### Shannon's paper

Claude E. Shannon, [*Prediction and Entropy of Printed English*](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1951.tb01366.x), *Bell System Technical Journal* 30(1), 50–64 (1951).

The paper estimates English entropy using experiments in which a human subject guesses the next **letter** based on preceding text. It documents the importance of context, grammar, idiom, and learned language statistics for prediction. It is a genuine conceptual predecessor of statistical language modeling, but it does not describe neural networks, transformer training, cognition as a whole, or consciousness.

The paper does not name its subject. Some secondary accounts say the subject was Shannon's wife, Betty, but the outline should either source that historical detail separately or leave it out.

### Empirical overlap—and its limits

- Martin Schrimpf et al., [*The Neural Architecture of Language: Integrative Modeling Converges on Predictive Processing*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105646118), *PNAS* (2021), reports that next-word-prediction performance across tested models correlates with their fit to neural and behavioral data during sentence processing. This is evidence relevant to the language system, not evidence that the brain is a transformer.
- Ariel Goldstein et al., [*Shared Computational Principles for Language Processing in Humans and Deep Language Models*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01026-4), *Nature Neuroscience* (2022), reports evidence of contextual next-word prediction, surprise, and context-specific semantic representation in both domains. The authors explicitly distinguish shared computational principles from identical internal mechanisms.
- Richard J. Antonello, [*Language Models: Does the Brain Really Know What Word Is Coming Next?*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.111163), *eLife* (2026), cautions that apparent future-word encoding can arise from correlations within language itself and therefore may not establish the predictive computation attributed to the brain.

## Source 5: Memory Through Planarian Regeneration

Tal Shomrat and Michael Levin, [*An Automated Training Paradigm Reveals Long-term Memory in Planarians and Its Persistence Through Head Regeneration*](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.087809), *Journal of Experimental Biology* 216(20), 3799–3810 (2013).

### What the study found

The authors used automated environmental-familiarization training. Trained planarians remembered the environment for at least 14 days. Headless fragments regenerated a brain; their direct post-regeneration recall difference was not statistically significant, but the authors found a significant **savings** effect after a re-exposure protocol: previously familiarized worms reacquired the behavior more quickly than controls.

### What it can support

The result supports a careful sentence: **some learning-relevant trace survived decapitation and brain regeneration in this planarian paradigm.** It motivates research into whether information can be stored in peripheral, bioelectric, epigenetic, or other bodily processes and integrated with the newly generated brain.

### What it cannot support

- It does not localize the memory trace outside the brain.
- The authors discuss possible peripheral-nervous-system contributions and do not rule them out.
- It does not demonstrate non-neural memory in humans or solve the mechanism of autobiographical recall.
- Michael Levin's broader [bioelectric-networks review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-023-01780-3) is an important theoretical framework, but it should not be cited as an independent replication of the 2013 behavioral result.

## The Barnum Effect: Analogy Only

The Barnum effect is the tendency to regard broad, generally applicable statements as uniquely personal. It is a useful reader-facing analogy for why an answer that repeats familiar strategy language may feel deeply tailored.

Do not write that the HBR researchers found the Barnum effect, because they did not measure it. Do not write that the models were “lying,” because neither a false statement nor intent to deceive is what the study operationalizes. Keep the article's vocabulary precise:

- **generic or context-insensitive** for advice that could fit many situations;
- **order-sensitive** for a result that changes when inputs are reordered;
- **sycophantic** only when a source specifically studies agreement with a user's belief; and
- **deceptive** only for research that operationalizes strategically misleading behavior.

## What the Research Does Not Prove

Neither source establishes any of the following:

- AI cannot produce novel ideas or creative artifacts.
- AI can never understand a value proposition.
- AI is conscious or definitively not conscious.
- AI intentionally manipulates an executive.
- Humans make unbiased strategic decisions.

The post should make a practical inference instead: these failure modes are enough to make unverified AI strategy advice a poor substitute for direct customer evidence, rival hypotheses, and explicit human accountability.

## Publication Checklist

- Link the Google and HBR primary sources, not just the videos.
- Keep the HBR figures attached to the authors' tested scenario; do not turn them into universal model properties.
- Keep “soul” and “soulless” in the title or voice, not as empirical terminology.
- Attribute the claim that opportunity begins in human experience to the author’s stated scope and philosophy, not to the two AI studies.
- Treat the listed theories of consciousness as research placeholders and state what architecture or test each would require. Avoid presenting any as a consensus test for AI qualia.
- Distinguish theoretical or metaphysical possibility from evidence: there is no validated bridge from an AI's computation, behavior, or self-report to a human-like phenomenal state. A current model's use of introspective language is not such a bridge.
- Attribute the whole-brain/LLM equivalence to Elan Barenholtz's hypothesis only after checking the intended source; current sources support a proposal about autoregressive language, not an established identical-algorithm finding.
- Describe Shannon's experiment as human next-letter prediction. The paper does not identify the participant as Shannon's spouse.
- Describe the Levin/planarian result as a memory-savings result after regeneration, not proof of human extra-brain memory or a settled storage location.
- Avoid claiming that neuroscience has established biological substrate as necessary for all consciousness.
- Treat the closing “prime mover” sequence as an explicitly speculative parable. It can illustrate the difference between records of a lived condition and participation in that condition; it is not evidence that future AI will lack experience or believe humans mythical.
- Fact-check every move from a metric to a human outcome with customer research, behavioral observation, or an appropriate experiment.
