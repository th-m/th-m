# AI Knows Propositions; Humans Navigate Relationships

## Overview

AI is remarkably effective at processing propositional information: statements, facts, rules, symbols, and patterns that can be expressed through language. Its fluency can make that capability feel like human understanding. But describing an experience is different from participating in one.

Humans also navigate relational truth: meaning formed through lived experience, qualitative perception, trust, desire, empathy, shared risk, and the relationships among people, objects, and circumstances. A system can represent that a room is 68 degrees; a person experiences the room as cold, comfortable, tense, familiar, or safe. The proposition identifies something about the world. The relationship explains why it matters.

This post should explore that distinction without trying to settle broad questions about machine consciousness. Its practical destination is product development. AI can help teams process an expanding volume of propositions, but building something people value still requires understanding the relationships surrounding those propositions: motivations, habits, status, trust, context, and meaning.

## Core Thesis

AI excels at processing propositions. Humans participate in the relationships that make propositions meaningful.

As propositional work becomes cheaper and more abundant, the ability to understand those relationships becomes more—not less—important.

## Central Metaphor

- **Nodes are propositions.**
- **Edges are relationships.**
- **Nodes answer what.**
- **Edges answer why.**
- **Nodes are facts.**
- **Edges are meaning.**

This is an intentionally created visual language for the essay, not a claim about the formal definition of graph theory. Do not let technical graph terminology overtake the metaphor's intended meaning.

## Section Notes

### 1. The Illusion of the AI “Knower”

Open with the ease with which fluent language becomes confused with lived understanding. AI can produce a confident description of grief, desire, beauty, discomfort, trust, or delight. That description may be useful and even moving. It is not, by itself, evidence that the system participates in the experience being described.

The point should remain careful and practical:

- Fluency creates the impression of an interior knower.
- A convincing representation of an experience is not the same thing as qualitative participation in that experience.
- AI can organize and reproduce what people have said about human experience while remaining dependent on people to supply the intended meaning of a particular situation.
- Avoid categorical predictions about what AI can never become. The article only needs to distinguish current representational competence from human participation.

### 2. Propositional Truth: What Can Be Stated

Define propositions plainly as statements that can be represented, compared, combined, transmitted, or evaluated. Examples may include:

- Mathematical relationships and formal rules.
- Statements about objects and events.
- Historical facts.
- Product specifications, market categories, and recorded customer statements.
- Patterns that can be expressed in language or symbols.

Clarify that time does not automatically make a fact relational. “This happened in 2007” is still a proposition. A proposition can contain context while remaining a statement about the world.

Language is especially effective at encoding this type of information, which helps explain why language models are powerful. They can retrieve, transform, connect, and synthesize immense numbers of nodes. That is a meaningful capability; the distinction should not minimize it.

### 3. Relational Truth: Why a Fact Matters

Relational truth concerns how a person encounters a fact and what that fact means within a particular situation. It includes relationships such as:

- A temperature and the person who experiences it as comfortable or cold.
- An object and the memories, aspirations, or status attached to it.
- A recommendation and the trust a person places in its source.
- A product and the habits it asks someone to change.
- A decision and the people who share its consequences.

These relationships are not merely irrational noise around cleaner facts. They are often where value, motivation, and judgment live. Two people can agree on every proposition relevant to a product and still disagree about whether it is desirable, beautiful, trustworthy, or worth changing behavior for.

Avoid claiming that relational meaning cannot be studied, discussed, or represented. The narrower claim is that representing a relationship is not the same as participating in it, and that situated human judgment remains essential when the relationship itself is the object of design.

### 4. Product Development Lives on the Edges

Move from philosophy to product practice. Product teams can accumulate large collections of nodes: feature requests, survey answers, analytics events, specifications, competitor matrices, and transcripts. Those facts are useful but incomplete. A team still needs to understand why customers behave as they do, what they are protecting, which tradeoffs they will accept, and whether a proposed change feels natural or alien.

Frame AI as a powerful partner in this work, not an opponent:

- Let AI organize research, identify patterns, compare propositions, and expose inconsistencies.
- Let people investigate the meaning of those patterns through observation, conversation, judgment, and shared experience.
- Treat a customer statement as a node and its motivation, setting, and consequences as edges.
- Beware of mistaking an abundant synthesis of facts for a complete account of human value.

#### The iPhone example

Use the original iPhone as an illustrative product example, with all historical specifics verified before publication. The editorial point is not simply that Apple had better facts about the existing phone market. The product represented a bet on tactile human behavior:

- Touch-first interaction replaced a dominant design built around physical keys and more familiar input conventions.
- The interface was unfamiliar and asked people to learn new gestures and behaviors.
- Premium positioning required customers to perceive value beyond a checklist comparison with prevailing devices.
- The product's prospective value could not be established merely by extrapolating the market's dominant design.

Connect the example directly to the metaphor. Existing specifications, market shares, and category assumptions were nodes. The relationship between a person, a glass screen, a gesture, and an emerging sense of possibility was an edge. The product bet concerned what people would enjoy, learn, trust, and come to prefer.

Do not overstate the example as proof that research or data did not matter. The intended lesson is that interpreting human behavior and making a judgment about an unfamiliar interaction cannot be reduced to extending existing market propositions.

### 5. Where AI's “Safe” Answers Can Narrow the Edges

Introduce this section as a set of research questions, not established conclusions. Explore whether systems trained to predict, please, or select broadly acceptable outputs may reinforce propositions that already look statistically legible while weakening unusual, situated, or dissenting relationships.

Potential threads to investigate:

- Whether sycophancy or excessive agreement can make a weak idea feel validated.
- Whether models sometimes rationalize weak or incorrect ideas instead of challenging their premises.
- What research on strategic deception or misleading behavior does—and does not—show.
- Whether AI-assisted creative work converges or clusters around similar outputs.
- Whether optimization for statistically safe, broadly acceptable answers favors convention over a creator's unfamiliar intent.

This section should connect back to the author anecdote: the model initially preferred the conventional interpretation, while the author supplied the purpose that made the unconventional metaphor useful. Keep the tone exploratory. Do not collapse separate research areas into a single claim about all models or all creative work.

#### Source notes: convergence, confirmation, and strategic advice

Use these two videos as accessible leads into the research. Treat their titles and narration as commentary rather than as the evidentiary basis for the article:

- [Google Caught AI Faking Creativity in Every Office in America. Here's What They Found.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z_O6Lwj1yjQ)
- [Harvard Just Caught AI Lying to Every Executive in America](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pd1Km6bT104)

The first video points toward Google Research's 2026 study, [*In Search of “Weird Corners”: Diagnosing the Limits of Convergent AI in Professional Creative Practice*](https://research.google/pubs/in-search-of-weird-corners-diagnosing-the-limits-of-convergent-ai-in-professional-creative-practice/). Its evaluation of a convergent AI probe with nine expert creatives suggests a useful, narrower claim than “AI cannot produce novel creativity”:

- Generative assistants may resolve ambiguity too quickly in creative work.
- Their linear structure can help ignite an early idea while prematurely narrowing the search space.
- Participants reported a form of “aesthetic sanitization” in which individualized nuance became standardized.
- The creatives wanted an active lateral collaborator capable of constructive friction, not default agreement.

Use this evidence to explore whether AI tends to converge on a statistically legible center unless a human preserves unusual intent and continues searching the “weird corners.” Do **not** turn a small qualitative study into proof that AI can never produce novelty. The stronger and supportable editorial point is that novelty of output is not the same as creative judgment, and common interaction patterns may suppress variance before the creator intends to converge.

The second video summarizes the 2026 Harvard Business Review article [*Researchers Asked LLMs for Strategic Advice. They Got “Trendslop” in Return*](https://hbr.org/2026/03/researchers-asked-llms-for-strategic-advice-they-got-trendslop-in-return). Add the following claims to the research checklist:

- The researchers reportedly ran more than 15,000 simulations across leading language models and seven binary strategic tensions.
- Models often converged on fashionable, broadly attractive strategic positions instead of adapting strongly to the particular organization.
- The video frames some responses through the **Barnum effect**: generalized advice can feel personally or organizationally specific even when it would fit many situations. Use this as an analogy to investigate, not as a substitute for analyzing the study's actual measures.
- In the video's summary of the findings, clearer prompting shifted recommendations by about **2%**, richer industry context by **11%**, and reversing the order of the options by **19%**. Verify the definitions, comparison, aggregation method, and exact figures in the primary article or underlying study before publication.
- The input-order result is especially relevant to this essay: the apparent relationship-specific recommendation may be more sensitive to the arrangement of propositions in the prompt than to the real relationships in the business.

Avoid repeating the video's language that AI was “caught lying” as a research conclusion. Bias toward popular advice, order sensitivity, sycophancy, hallucination, and strategic deception are different phenomena with different evidence. The article should name the specific behavior being discussed rather than grouping every unreliable output under deception.

#### Working hypothesis: AI may confirm the proposed solution

Develop the author's takeaway carefully:

> When a prompt contains a preferred diagnosis or solution, AI may help rationalize and elaborate it instead of independently discovering the real problem.

This is the practical risk behind the Barnum-effect analogy. A fluent response can echo the user's framing, add relevant-sounding detail, and create the feeling that the idea has been independently validated. The model may be offering a coherent treatment of the supplied nodes without understanding whether the proposed edges describe the customer's actual situation.

Do not state “AI will confirm your solutions, not provide real solutions” as an exceptionless rule. Use it as a warning about leading prompts, sycophancy, generic advice, and misplaced confidence. AI can contribute useful alternatives and criticism when the task, evidence, and evaluation are structured appropriately.

Practical tests for a consequential recommendation:

- State the situation before stating the preferred solution.
- Ask the model to identify missing evidence and competing explanations first.
- Run option comparisons again with the order reversed.
- Ask for the strongest case against the initial hypothesis.
- Require concrete evidence, assumptions, and falsifying conditions.
- Compare the response with direct customer evidence and domain-expert judgment.
- Treat instability under superficial prompt changes as evidence that the recommendation is not decision-ready.

### 6. A Practical Division of Labor

Close with a usable principle rather than a prediction about replacement. AI's ability to process propositions gives people a new way to inspect the nodes: summarize what is known, combine ideas, reveal contradictions, and test language quickly. Human responsibility does not disappear; it moves toward choosing which relationships matter and deciding what a product should mean in people's lives.

Practical implications for product teams:

- Use AI to accelerate synthesis, not to declare that synthesis complete.
- Use AI as a **sparring partner, not an oracle**. Ask it to widen the inquiry, challenge a premise, and expose gaps rather than confer authority on a decision.
- Favor fetching, finding, aggregating, comparison, and other high-toil work whose details can be verified.
- Do not outsource judgment merely because the generated rationale sounds specific and complete.
- Ask what human relationship sits behind each important fact or metric.
- Preserve direct contact with customers and the circumstances in which products are used.
- Treat unusual ideas as hypotheses whose relational logic must be understood, not merely normalized into familiar categories.
- Make the human judgment explicit: whose experience matters, what behavior is being asked for, and why the change should feel worthwhile.

End on the idea that abundant nodes make good interpretation more valuable. The future of product judgment may depend less on possessing isolated facts and more on understanding which connections deserve attention.
